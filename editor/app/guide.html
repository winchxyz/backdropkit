BackdropKit editor guide

How to make a theme for the Claude Code desktop app, one task at a time. Each part is a few steps with the names of the buttons as the editor shows them.

Point at any control in the editor to read what it does. Double-click a slider to reset it.

1. Basics

A theme is a pixel scene that plays behind your conversation in the app: a grid of 320 x 200 cells, one scene for each moment of the day (Day, Sunset, Night…), with motion on top. The editor builds it from layers and exports it ready to install.

The four areas of the screen

  1. Left panel: the tools at the top, then Layers, then Your art (every picture you brought in).
  2. Right panel, the inspector: five tabs, Layer, Look, Colour, Motion and Effects, for the selected layer.
  3. The day line at the bottom: the time, the moment's name, Play the day, the check (for example 14,363 of 131,072 · ok) and Export.
  4. The top: the menus (File, Edit, View, Help), the theme's name, Guide, Show chat and Play. Tab hides the panels to see the scene alone.

Layers

  1. Layers stack: the top one in the list covers the ones below. Drag a row up or down to move it, or use its ••• menu: Move up, Move down.
  2. Click a layer to edit it in the inspector. Under each name the list says when it shows: All day, Night only, Day, Sunset.
  3. The eye on a row hides the layer at every moment. Click it again to show it.
  4. To show a layer at some moments only: Layer tab → Shows at, click a moment's chip to turn it off there. A star field at Night only, say.

The day line

  1. Click or drag along the day line: the scene shows that time. [ and ] step to the previous and next moment.
  2. The handles between two moments set when each one starts. Drag one; the hour shows as you drag.
  3. Play the day runs the whole day, an hour a second.

2. Remix one of the 14 themes

  1. On the start, choose One of the 14 themes and pick one. (Later: File → New….) It comes in as one Picture layer, held still, with its picture for each moment.
  2. Click + next to Layers and choose Clouds. Clouds appear above the picture; the Layer tab has their Clouds, Size, Shape, Colour, Highest and Lowest, and New shapes.
  3. Open the Motion tab and set Moves by to Drift. One loop is how many seconds a cloud takes to cross. Press M to see the motion in the editor.
  4. For stars: + → Stars. They come in twinkling, shown at the night moments only. Change that in Shows at.
  5. Press P to play the scene exactly as the app will get it; Esc stops. Then Export (part 12).

3. One picture into a theme

  1. On the start, choose My own art and pick one picture: PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF. You can also drop it anywhere on the page, or paste it.
  2. The picture fills the scene the way the app fills a window, and the Look tab opens. A look decides how the picture becomes cells. Looks shows ready-made ones on your own picture: Clean pixels, Soft dither, Night grain, Poster and more. Click one.
  3. Below the looks, each section changes one part: Background, Picture, Colours, Dither, Pixel shapes, Glyphs, Outline, Mirror. Hold \ (or Hold to compare) to see the original.
  4. To take the background away: Background → Take away. Auto finds it by itself; Colour takes away a colour you pick (with Tolerance and Soft edge); Dark or Light takes away everything dark or light. What is taken away shows the layers below.
  5. To place it: Layer tab → Your art: Across, Down, Size, and Flip, Cover, Fit. Or with the Move tool (V): drag it, pull a corner, or hold Alt and scroll over it.
  6. Motion and effects: the Motion and Effects tabs (part 10).
  7. When the check on the day line says ok, Export (part 12).

4. Day and night versions of one picture

Two pictures of the same place, one by day and one by night, become one layer that switches picture with the time.

From the start

  1. Choose My own art and select both pictures at once (Ctrl-click, or ⌘-click on a Mac).
  2. winch-ai asks whether they are the same scene at different times of day. Each picture has a moment under it, guessed from how bright it is: the brightest is Day, the darkest Night.
  3. Click a moment to change it. Choosing a moment another picture has swaps the two.
  4. The line under them gives the hours. Two pictures make Day 06:00–18:00 and Night 18:00–06:00; three or four add Sunrise or Sunset in between.
  5. Choose Yes, one scene. One layer holds both pictures, in the same place and with one look. The list shows it as Day / Night · 2 pictures.
  6. Drag along the day line: at 18:00 the night picture takes over. Drag the handle between Day and Night to change that hour.

No, separate pieces makes one layer per picture instead, each shown all day.

By hand, on a layer you already have

  1. Select the picture's layer. In the Layer tab, under Times of day, click Add a version for another time of day… (it is also in the layer's ••• menu).
  2. Choose the moment, for example For Night, then the night picture.
  3. It goes in the same place with the same look and shows only at Night; the first picture stops showing there. Times of day lists each picture with its moments; the ••• on a row changes them or removes the version.
  4. If the picture is already in Your art: go to that moment on the day line, right-click its thumbnail and choose Show it in the layer at Night.

One look covers all the pictures. To give each its own look, Times of day → Split into layers: each picture becomes a layer shown at its moments. Versions are still pictures (a GIF gives its first frame), and they keep their own colours, so Light by the time of day is off for them.

5. More times of day

  1. Click + Moment under the day line. A moment goes into the longest stretch of the day with a usual name (Sunrise, Evening…). Every colour is relit for it; your art keeps its colours.
  2. To rename it, click the moment's name next to the time and choose Rename, or double-click its stretch of the day line.
  3. Drag the handles on the day line to set when each moment starts. A theme has up to six.
  4. To remove one, click its name and choose Remove.
  5. One picture per moment: on the start, choose My own art with up to six pictures and give each its moment (Sunrise, Morning, Day, Sunset, Evening, Night). Or add a version for each moment by hand (part 4).

6. Colours that follow the time of day

Every colour in the palette has its own shade at each moment. Generator layers (sky, hills, trees…) are lit by the sun layer at each moment by themselves.

  1. Your art keeps its own colours all day. To have it dim at night: Layer tab → Your art → Light by the time of day. It is taken as drawn by day: warmer at sunset, darker and bluer at night.
  2. Colour tab → Colours at the moment on show. Click a swatch: Colour lists its shade at every moment, and you can set any of them. Relight from this moment fills its other moments from this shade.
  3. Relight: choose the light each moment gets (dawn is pink, sunset warm, night blue and much darker), then Relight every colour. Every colour's other moments are filled from the moment on show. Generators keep their own.
  4. Tidy: Merge similar joins colours that look alike at every moment; Remove unused drops colours no layer uses.

7. Sprites and GIFs

  1. Drop a sprite (a PNG with a clear background) or a GIF on the page, or + → Your art…. A sprite stands in the lower middle at a fair size.
  2. Move tool (V): drag it; pull a corner to size it; or hold Alt and scroll over it. The Layer tab has Across, Down, Size and Flip (Shift+H).
  3. A GIF plays its frames. Layer tab → Frames → Speed, in frames per second (20 is the fastest the app plays). , and . step through the frames.
  4. To send it across the scene: Motion → Moves by → Drift, then Direction and One loop. Fall drops it and brings it back on top; Sway and Bob rock it.
  5. Several pictures at once go to Your art at the left: click one to add it as a layer.

8. From scratch with the generators

  1. On the start, choose A blank scene, then the moment to start in. A quiet valley: a sky, a sun and moon, mountains, hills, pines and clouds, each one a layer drawn by a generator.
  2. Select a layer; its settings are in the Layer tab. Sky: Colours, Bands, Horizon, Dither. Ridges and hills: Shape, Foot, Height, Rough, Distance, Colour by day, Snow. The New … button draws new shapes with the same settings.
  3. Sun and moon: Across and Height are set for the moment on show. Hills, clouds and water take their light from it.
  4. + adds more: Sky, Sun and moon, Stars, Clouds, Ridges and hills, Trees, Water.
  5. To paint on a generated layer: To paint in the Layer tab. Its generator stops there.

9. Painting cell by cell

  1. + → Paint adds a paint layer and picks the Pencil.
  2. The tools at the top left: Move V, Pencil B, Eraser E, Fill G, Line L (Shift: level or 45°), Rectangle R (Shift: filled), Pick colour I (or hold Alt with any tool). 1 2 3 set the brush size.
  3. Choose the colour in the Colour tab, or Alt-click it in the scene. A colour has its own shade at each moment, so one stroke looks right all day.
  4. On a layer with a picture for each moment (a theme's picture, say): Paint every moment at once on, a stroke goes to all of them; off, only to the moment on show.
  5. Undo Ctrl+Z (⌘Z), redo Ctrl+Y (⇧⌘Z). Zoom with + and −, F fits the scene, hold Space to pan. View → Cell grid when close shows the cells when zoomed in.

10. Motion and live effects

Motion: the Motion tab, Moves by

  • Drift: slides the layer across and around, wrapping at the edge. Clouds. One loop (20 to 600 s) and Direction.
  • Fall: drops it and brings it back on top. Snow, rain. One loop.
  • Sway and Bob: rock it side to side or up and down by 1 to 3 cells. Grass, a boat. Period, Amount.
  • Twinkle: fades parts of it in turn. Stars, glints on water.

Live effects: the Effects tab

  • Colour cycle: the layer's colours step through each other in order. Water, neon, fire.
  • Pulse: the layer breathes, its opacity easing down and back. Lights, glow.
  • Glint: a narrow band of light sweeps across now and then. Not on a layer with frames, and the layer must be at least three cells thick.

What they cost

The app's player draws every moving layer again on every frame, and motion and effects add a little to the scene's size. A few small moving layers cost little; a large layer that moves costs the most. Click the check on the day line to see each moment and the layers that cost the most there.

M previews motion in the editor; P plays the real exported scene. The app plays motion only with the GPU on and reduced motion off; otherwise it shows a still frame.

11. "Too big" or "may stutter"

The check on the day line reads, for example, 14,363 of 131,072 · ok: the largest moment, in characters, of the most the app's player accepts in one scene.

  • Too big: a moment is over 131,072 characters. The exporter first merges near colours (up to 6 levels on a channel) and says so; if it is still over, it cannot export.
  • May stutter: the check estimates the time the app needs to draw each frame. Over 2.78 ms it warns ("It may stutter on slower computers"); over 5.56 ms it is an error.

Click the check: every moment has a meter and its three costliest layers. Click a layer's name to select it.

  1. Make it fit (Look tab, on art that takes much of a scene): simplifies the look a step at a time until the art takes at most 60% of a scene, or what the other layers leave. A lighter dither, a smoother picture, fewer colours. Undo brings it back.
  2. Fewer colours: Look → Colours → Colours lower, or Colour tab → Merge similar.
  3. Smaller art: a lower Size, or Background → Take away so less is drawn. Flat areas and ordered patterns cost less than diffusion: the Dither → Pattern list marks each one cheap, medium or costly.
  4. Fewer moving layers: set Moves by to None on the ones that matter least, or hide a layer at the moments it is not needed (Shows at).

12. Export and install

  1. Click Export on the day line (or Ctrl+E, ⌘E). winch-ai asks for the Name, the Folder name (small letters, numbers and dashes; the theme installs under it) and a Line about it. Click Export.
  2. It encodes every moment, measures the light of each, takes the preview and packs the archive. The archive, named after the folder (my-theme.zip), goes to your downloads.
  3. Inside: .claude-plugin/marketplace.json, mod/.claude-plugin/plugin.json, mod/hooks/ (the theme mod), preview.png, project.json and README.md.
  4. Click Copy on the Install prompt and give it to Claude Code in the app, with the archive in your downloads. It lists what is inside, reads the mod, unpacks it into ~/.claude/backdropkit/my-theme and runs: 
    claude plugin marketplace add "<that folder>"
claude plugin install my-theme@backdropkit-my-theme --scope user
    Then run /reload-plugins or start a new session.
  5. The theme's own commands, as its README lists them (with your folder name in place of my-theme):
    • /my-theme-time lists when each scene plays. /my-theme-time night (another moment, or a time like 18:30) previews it and runs on from it; /my-theme-time now goes back to your clock.
    • /my-theme-off takes the theme off, in new sessions too; /my-theme-on puts it back.
    • To remove it: claude plugin uninstall my-theme@backdropkit-my-theme.
  6. To update a theme: open its project (part 13), change it, export again with the same folder name and give Claude Code the new install prompt. It replaces the older copy in the same folder. Then /reload-plugins.

The README in the archive also says how to install by hand. If another theme plugin is on, the theme set last wins.

13. Save, open, carry on

  1. As you work, the editor keeps the project in this browser. Saved shows next to the name. Reload the page and it comes back.
  2. File → Save project (Ctrl+S, ⌘S) downloads my-theme.project.json: your art, layers, colours and moments.
  3. File → Open project… (Ctrl+O, ⌘O), or Open a project on the start, opens a project.json or a theme archive made here.
  4. File → New… shows the start; Back to your theme returns to it.

The browser keeps one project. Save a file for each theme you want to keep.

15. Short questions

Why does everything become pixels?
The app plays pixel scenes only. A scene is 320 x 200 cells, each one colour, and the app's player takes at most 131,072 characters of it. Your art becomes cells; the look decides how.
Why do my colours shift?
Each colour has a shade for every moment. Generators and relit colours get warmer at sunset and darker and bluer at night. Your art keeps its own colours unless Light by the time of day is on. Merging near colours (Merge similar, Make it fit, or the exporter fitting a large scene) can also nudge them a little.
Can I share a theme?
Yes. The archive is the whole theme: anyone can install it with its install prompt, and its project.json opens in the editor to change it. Share only art you are free to share.
Why do the editor's panels stay dark when the scene is bright?
The panels keep one tone while you work, dark by default, so they do not flip as you move along the day. View → Panels: Dark, Light, or Follow the scene (light panels over a light scene and dark over a dark one, as the app does with an installed theme).
Where is my work?
In this browser, on your computer. Nothing is uploaded. Save a project file to keep a copy.
